\section*{SPICE vs Spingarn on three crossbar models}

\paragraph{Setup.}
Each case is an $N\times N$ crossbar with source voltages $E\in[-1, 1.5]$\,V, per-cell $R_{\min}$ log-uniform in
1--10\,k$\Omega$ and $R_{\max}$ in 50--200\,k$\Omega$, 5\,$\Omega$ wires, 50\,$\Omega$ sources and 1\,k$\Omega$ loads.
The cell is a tanh memristor alone (\emph{memristor}), or a memristor in series with an ideal or a Shockley diode
($I_s = 10^{-9}$\,A, $n = 1$) on a branch of its own through an internal node (\emph{1D1R}). Every size has ten random
circuits (seeds 0--9), solved by both solvers. SPICE is ngspice-47 with KLU and RELTOL $10^{-3}$; it writes the ideal
diode as $\mathrm{uramp}(V)/1\,\mathrm{m\Omega}$ and the Shockley diode as a behavioural $\exp$ source. Spingarn keeps
its port resistances fixed (no adaptive re-matching) and factorizes with CHOLMOD in its own (AMD) ordering, with
$\alpha = 1$ on the memristor and $0.8$ on 1D1R; zero-bias $\gamma = R_{\min}$ per memristor and, on 1D1R, the diode
port $\sqrt{R_{\min}R_{\max}}$ per cell. Both stop on the same KCL test. Each run executed in its own process on one
machine (Intel i5-11600KF, 31.8\,GB, single-threaded BLAS); runtime is the whole solve. A run still solving after
1600\,s was stopped and counts as failed to converge within the time limit. SPICE was not run at $1024\times1024$,
where ngspice's KLU fails on these circuits regardless of time.

\paragraph{Results} (medians over the ten circuits; Tables~1--2, Figures~1--5).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Memristor.} SPICE converged by plain Newton in 5 iterations at every size; Spingarn's median stayed at 19--22
  iterations at every size up to $1024\times1024$. Spingarn was faster at every size: 3.9$\times$ at
  $16\times16$, 12$\times$ at $512\times512$ (3.69 against 44.2\,s; 0.72 against 3.09\,GB), and solved
  $1024\times1024$ in 15.9\,s (range 15.5--16.3\,s, 2.83\,GB).
  \item \emph{1D1R, ideal diode.} SPICE took a median of 8--16 Newton iterations, plain Newton in 59 of its 60 runs (one
  $16\times16$ circuit needed gmin stepping). Spingarn's iterations stay at 113--130 up to $128\times128$ and then grow
  about threefold per doubling of $N$ (170, 496, 1596 at 256, 512, 1024). It ties SPICE at $16\times16$ and is
  3.1$\times$ faster at 256 and 512 (90.6 against 279\,s; 0.98 against 4.59\,GB); at $1024\times1024$ it took
  1169\,s (1144--1233\,s), every run within the cap.
  \item \emph{1D1R, Shockley diode.} SPICE needed gmin stepping on every run (medians of 78--94 iterations) and was 1.4$\times$
  slower than Spingarn at $16\times16$, 35$\times$ slower at $256\times256$ (284 against 8.2\,s; 4.6 against
  0.30\,GB). On one $128\times128$ circuit ngspice reported success after 1255 iterations of gmin stepping, but its
  operating point failed the KCL test (relative residual $2.6\times10^{-3}$, above RELTOL), so it counts as not
  converged. At $512\times512$ the first circuit was still unsolved at the 1600\,s cap; KLU's reordering had taken the
  process to a peak of at least 19\,GB of RAM and 27\,GB committed, leaving the 32\,GB machine almost without free
  memory, so the other nine circuits were not run. Spingarn took the same iterations as with the ideal diode (158,
  486, 1610 at 256, 512, 1024): 110\,s at $512\times512$, at least 14.6$\times$ faster than SPICE, and 1445\,s
  (1418--1479\,s) at $1024\times1024$, every run within the cap.
  \item \emph{Agreement.} The two solvers' word- and bit-line potentials differ by at most $1.2\times10^{-10}$\,V on the
  memristor and $2.2\times10^{-7}$\,V on the ideal diode (SPICE's $1\,\mathrm{m\Omega}$ diode). On the Shockley diode
  they agree to $7.5\times10^{-8}$\,V or better except on two circuits where SPICE stopped with a looser KCL residual
  (within RELTOL), $6.6\times10^{-7}$ and $7.8\times10^{-6}$\,V.
\end{itemize}
